What Foster taught me

  • Design from both sides, or the whole thing stalls. We built from the employer's point of view without enough customer conversation, and missed that colleges ran their own institutional decision cycle.
  • Narrative is a growth lever, not decoration. We moved from selling a placement management tool to a college discovery tool for employers, and college-side adoption went up.
  • Effort scales linearly. Systems scale exponentially. The supply side became a system. The parts that stayed manual never compounded.
  • Resourced to exist is not resourced to win. Enough to keep going is not enough to break through. Constraints can kill a product faster than the market does.

Agentic AI for video generation.

7–8 months  ·  0 → Prototype

ResultPrototype0 → working build, over 7–8 months

Limitations

  • The outcome was qualitative, in a complex way, Even a technically correct agent pipeline could not guarantee output a client would accept. The complexity ran deeper than I anticipated.
  • I built before walking the process manually, I started building around image and video models without first doing the manual R&D myself. An early mistake, learnt late.
  • The ground kept shifting, Image and video models were evolving too fast to build a stable product on top of them.

What I learned

  • Validate feasibility, not just demand, Customer conversations proved the gap was real. Only hands-on R&D could have proved the build. Do both before committing.
  • Building a product is a different craft from taking it to market, Years of SaaS GTM did not translate into product development. I now know first-hand where my craft ends.
  • Pressure turns thinkers into executors, The pressure I put on myself to keep things moving had me executing instead of thinking. I watch for that now.
