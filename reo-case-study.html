01

What Reo.Dev sells.

For technical products, buying intent shows up in developer behaviour, GitHub activity, package installs, docs usage, CLI runs, long before it shows up in a CRM. Whoever captures that signal first owns the pipeline.

Positioned today as The Technical GTM Mesh

The problem Reo.Dev solves

An engineer finds a tool and installs it. The team adopts it quietly. The org evaluates. Only at procurement does the CRM see anything.

Stage 1

Engineer discovers, installs, pulls, clones

Stage 2

Team adopts quietly through docs, CLI and trials

Stage 3

Org evaluates, still invisible to sales

Stage 4

Procurement surfaces, the CRM finally sees it

Traditional intent tools only see that last stage. The real evaluation happens above it, invisible. So sales chases cold accounts while warm ones evaluate in silence.

02

What the content actually does.

This is a category-led content engine, not a blog. The positioning is deliberately narrow, GTM for technical buyers and nothing wider, and every property carries the same prefix. Content is treated as the layer that builds the category, not as lead-gen support for sales.

Four things the engine does

01

Creates demand instead of capturing it

Ranks for adjacent problems buyers already search for, then leads them to the bigger reframe. See How to monetize your OSS, the DevRel Guide, and Why 90% of Developer Content Fails.

02

Makes the product feel necessary

Content teaches plays that require the product’s infrastructure. The need gets created without the product being sold. See the playbook Identify Developers Opening Issues & Comments on your Competitor GitHub Repositories.

03

Borrows credibility

Co-creating with recognised DevTool operators transfers that authority. See the DevGTM Ecosystem hub and the Unstructured.io session on converting OSS signals into pipeline.

04

Owns the vocabulary

If DevGTM becomes the category language, competitors have to play on Reo’s terms. DevGTM Blog, Playbooks, Academy, Signals Guide, Modern DevGTM Newsletter.

03

How the content gets distributed.

The LinkedIn page is not the channel. It is an aggregator.

Only 1 of the last 11 posts on the company page was original, an employee spotlight. Everything else was a repost. The restructure started five to six months ago.

This is deliberate. LinkedIn suppresses company pages and rewards personal profiles, so the entire broadcast function has moved onto people and the page just collects it.

What the page amplifies

The team

CEO on category narratives and provocative POVs. Co-founders on product depth, podcasts and demos. Marketing on launch teasers and campaigns.

A fictional “Co-CEO” with its own account runs humour and product disguised as memes.

Industry operators

DevTools GTM experts who reinforce Reo’s positioning.

Repeated narratives suggest these talking points are seeded, not purely organic.

Customers and community

DevGTM Academy graduates and customers supplying social proof and community-generated content.

Three tactics doing the work

Comment gating

Comment “Agent” produced 47 comments against 53 reactions, deliberately optimising for reach and lead capture over vanity engagement.

Leadership engagement

Executives are consistently among the first reactors, which builds early post velocity.

Naming discipline

#ReoLaunchMonth, #AgentGTM and DevGTM used consistently to compound the category.

This is not a company-page content machine. It is a people-powered distribution network, with employees, operators, customers and community members doing the broadcasting.
